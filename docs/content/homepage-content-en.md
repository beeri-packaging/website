## From Need to Finished Box

### The Customer Need

The customer arrives with a product, a marketing goal and often practical limits such as weight, refrigeration, display or shipping. The first step is understanding what the packaging must solve before design begins.

### Development and Design

A professional team reviews the product, dimensions, materials, opening experience and shelf presence. From there, structural and visual ideas are developed to fit the brand, budget, quantity and production process.

### Precise Packaging

The result is packaging tailored to the packed product: structure, strength, size, print and finishing. Good packaging protects the product, presents it clearly and strengthens the customer experience at every touchpoint.

## Materials and Finishes

Material choice depends on the product, weight, storage conditions, shipping method and the brand feeling the packaging needs to create.

### Common Material Types

- Folding carton for product packaging, cosmetics, pharma and shelf-ready packs.
- Corrugated or reinforced board for heavier products, shipping packs and packaging that needs higher durability.
- Rigid board or premium structures for gift packs, wine, cosmetics and products where the opening experience matters.
- Inserts, dividers and inner trays for protection, presentation and separation between products.
- Coatings, lamination and protective layers when better resistance to wear, moisture or extended handling is needed.

### Possible Finishes

- Selective varnish to highlight a logo, image or specific area on the package.
- Gold, silver or colored foil for a premium look.
- Embossing or debossing to add depth and a tactile feel.
- Shaped cutting, windows or special openings to showcase the product.
- Inner printing for a richer opening experience.

### Important Note for Food and Pharma Products

When packaging is intended for food, pharma or another sensitive product, it is important to define whether the package has direct product contact or serves only as outer packaging. This affects material choice, coatings and required checks.

## FAQ

### Do we need to come with a finished design?

Not necessarily. The process can start with an idea, an existing product, measurements or a business need. A finished design file can shorten the process, but proper packaging planning starts with the product first.

### What should we send to receive a quote?

It is useful to send product dimensions, weight, desired quantity, packaging use, storage and shipping conditions, preferred print type, inspiration examples and, if available, design files or a dieline.

### What is a dieline?

A dieline is the flat technical layout of the package before folding and assembly. It shows cut lines, fold lines, glue areas and the surfaces where the graphic design will be placed.

### What is die-cutting?

Die-cutting is the process of cutting packaging material into a defined shape. In carton packaging, it creates the exact box structure, openings, windows and folds.

### What is the difference between digital and offset printing?

Digital printing is often useful for flexibility, shorter runs, quick adjustments and personalization. Offset printing is usually better for larger quantities, high color consistency and efficient unit cost at scale.

### Can we receive a sample before full production?

In many projects, a sample or prototype is recommended before full production. It helps check size, opening, closing, stability, appearance and user experience before moving into a larger quantity.

### How do we choose the right packaging material?

Start with the product: weight, size, fragility, storage conditions, shipping method, point of sale and brand feeling. Then choose the material, thickness, structure and finish that fit the real use case.

### Is carton suitable for a heavy product?

Yes, but it needs proper planning. A heavy product requires the right material, reinforced structure, load distribution and sometimes inner dividers or supports. Carton should not be chosen by appearance alone.

### Can packaging be produced for a product series?

Yes. In that case, it is important to build a consistent brand language while adapting size, color, flavor, model or category. This keeps the series connected while making each product easy to identify.

### What affects the price of branded packaging?

Price is affected by package size, material, quantity, print method, number of colors, structural complexity, finishing, die preparation, gluing, samples, logistics and timelines.

### How long does packaging production take?

The timeline depends on project complexity, material availability, dieline approval, artwork approval, sample preparation and production quantity. That is why early briefing is recommended whenever possible.

### Can packaging become part of the brand experience?

Yes. In premium packaging, wine, cosmetics and gift packs, the package is often the customer’s first physical meeting with the brand. Comfortable opening, the right material and precise finishing can raise the perceived value of the product.
